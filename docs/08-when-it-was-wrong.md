# When It Was Wrong

Chapters 01–07 were prevention. This chapter is the day the habit looked
the other way, and you acted on — or repeated — an answer that turned out
to be confidently, distinctly, *undeniably* wrong. It happens to everyone
who uses these tools seriously, including the careful. This chapter is
the recovery manual: clean, kind, no panic required.

## First, the only rule

> **Fix the situation first. Fix the process second. Forgive yourself
> always.**

In that order, every time. Not "what should I have noticed?" ten seconds
after you realized a person was exposed to the wrong number or a medical
claim — the damage path matters most while it's still moving.

## The recovery ladder

### Rung 1: Contain (as long as it takes)

Ask: **who has the wrong version now?**

- Only you discovered it privately → rewrite. Done.
- You told one person → send the corrected version *forwarded visibly*:
  one message, plain words, the fix named. "Update — I said the fee was
  $40; turns out it's $75; please un-bake that from your shopping trip."
- It went into the group chat, a document, a meeting → same, at the
  place it landed: a follow-up thread, a tracked change, a one-liner
  emailed after. Speed matters more than eloquence everywhere in rung 1.
- It had stakes for someone else's body/records/money → and the person is
  reachable and unharmed → same. If anyone acted on it and could be
  hurt, the professionals from chapter 07 belong in this rung, not
  "later." One honest correction beats a beautiful silence; principles
  are the same for medical-bill errors, spoiled appointments, or a
  family member's wrong dose being *possible*.

### Rung 2: Diagnose (five minutes, once)

One single pass, with the taxonomy out (chapter 02): **which mistake was
it?** Stale? Numeric? Invented citation? The type tells you which *small
change* to your own habit plugs the hole — and notice how small these
usually are:

- **"I assumed the row was low."** The ask was actually row 3/4, and the
  habit's trigger is *row assignment*, not model outputs → the fix lives
  in the two-line stakes test (chapter 03).
- **"I checked the vibe, not the detail."** You re-read it twice instead
  of opening a source → the fix is the exact-quote search (chapter 05).
- **"One model said it, one stunningly fluent paragraph."** → the second
  opinion (chapter 06), applied to exactly this genre of question from
  now on.
- **"I asked leadingly."** Sycophancy bent your prompt → neutral prompts,
  forever (chapter 01's counter-habit).

If the diagnosis takes more than five minutes or hurts, quit the session
there: rung 1 skills will keep people safe without the ceremony. Most of
the time, your fix will be one habit adjustment, not a personality
rebuild.

### Rung 3: Repair the loop (where relevant)

The AI doesn't hold grudges, and your chat thread is workable:

- **Telling it what failed** ("that citation doesn't exist — please redo
  with only sources you're sure you know") re-orients the model's next
  attempts. Use it for the *next* ask, not to relitigate the previous
  one.
- **When a long chain went wrong early**, don't patch — restart the chain
  (taxonomy mistake #12). Take one retry at the same answer; the second
  attempt with a correction is often where big wins live.
- **Fresh chats for fresh topics** when the thread has gone sour, for
  your clarity more than the model's.

### Rung 4: Restore proportion (the part people forget)

The emotional head-fog after being fooled-by-AI is usually *heavier* than
the mistake. Calibrate here:

- This guide is an entire manual about it, and even so: **most AI mistakes
  are cheap**, chapter 03's rows 1–2 being most of them.
- You're in the same boat as everyone who uses these tools — reporters,
  librarians, nurses, engineers — and the calm stories you've heard this
  week about "I got burned by AI" share one theme: the person seems
  *strangely fine* after fixing it.
- If the same fact had come wrong out of a person's mouth, nobody would
  audit their trust in all of humanity. Extend your AI habit the smaller
  courtesy: one rung of diagnosis, one small repair, done.

## Fix forward, in one habit

The most valuable post-mistake move isn't a rescue: it's turning the
moment into a tells-you-x drill:

- **This week**: when the same *genre* of question reappears (taxes,
  dosage, procurement), run the second-model or source check first —
  one small fire drill on a live question beats ten hypotheticals.
- **This month**: tell the story forward. Telling one family member or
  coworker "here's how I noticed" is how your checker-network grows —
  and by chapter 09's logic, groups monkey-see-check better than they
  follow rules.

:::note Being wrong with AI beats being wrong alone
One more reframe, and a real one: the checking habit you're building is
*protecting* the people who trust you more each week. Someone who
verified once and was re-verified once is a safer node than someone who
never checks, and a calmer one than someone who distrusts everything. The
goal was never zero mistakes — it's *detectable, correctable, honest*
ones, and that's the house you now keep.
:::

## The calm summary

Contain the error to its travelers. Diagnose it one pass, privately,
against the taxonomy. Repair the loop if it's live. Forgive yourself by
remembering this is the shared cost of a powerful tool, that fixes here
are rarely expensive, and that your corrected word is the most credible
thing in every conversation it has ever appeared in.
