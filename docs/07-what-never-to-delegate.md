# What Never to Delegate

The previous chapters were about *how much* to check. This one is about
the blacklist that sits above all of it: the parts of life where AI help
is genuinely valuable — and where AI must never hold the pen.

The rule in one line:

> **AI may be your copilot anywhere.
> In law, medicine, and money, it never flies the plane.**

## The three big no-delegate domains

### Medicine (including anyone else's body)

Work with an AI freely to: understand what a medical word means, prepare
a list of questions for the appointment, organize your symptoms timeline,
read a discharge summary into plain English, remember what the pharmacist
said.

Never hand over: **doses and interactions** ("can I take X with my
meds?"), decisions to stop, start, or change treatment, triage of
symptoms ("is this an emergency?"), or anything about a child's or
dependent's care.

A model's answer here is *background for a professional*, in the
strongest sense of background: it earns nothing, claims nothing, decides
nothing. The professional has your actual chart, your actual history,
their actual license — each of those alone outweighs any answer ever
generated. Nurses, doctors, and pharmacists are the source of record.

> If you take one thing from this manual into your health life: let AI
> make you a *better patient*, never your own clinician.

### Law (including immigration, custody, and contracts)

Work with an AI freely to: learn what legal vocabulary means, understand
what a court or filing process *is*, organize your paperwork, draft
questions for the consult, prepare a timeline of events, rehearse what
you want to tell the attorney. Rules differ by state and change often —
which is exactly a model's worst zone (stale facts + rare details +
high stakes all at once).

Never hand over: **your specific legal decisions** — what to file, what
to sign, whether to appear, how to plead, what a clause "really means"
before you sign it, what your rights are in a dispute.

The free legal-aid clinics across the U.S. exist precisely for this.
A thirty-minute consult with a real attorney or legal-aid office beats
months of confident AI drafting, and hiring one **before** you sign or
file is cheaper than fixing it after.

### Money (including taxes, benefits, and retirement)

Work with an AI freely to: understand what an APR is, decode a fee
schedule's fine print, compare what two account types *generally* mean,
organize your receipts, draft the story of your finances, rehearse your
questions for the tax preparer or advisor.

Never hand over: **what to invest in or sell**, whether you personally
qualify for a benefit or deduction, the final tax answer, how much to
withdraw in retirement, or a "should I buy this house/car" verdict.

Big stakes, personal specifics, and — the part that matters most for
this guide — **numbers that are silently wrong-able**: a misremembered
tax bracket or a wrong sign in retirement math is mistake type #3 (the
numeric slip) with dollar signs attached. If the AI answer has any money
in it, the *AI Scam Safety Guide* rule applies even before delegation: no
amount, no account details, no order given in the same sitting the idea
arrived.

## The common thread across all three

Notice *why* these three are the blacklist, and what the smaller ones
have in common:

| Domain | Why it's blacklisted | And its cousins |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medicine | wrong answer hurts a body | anything about someone's safety |
| Law | wrong answer can cost rights, custody, immigration status | anything with a deadline in court |
| Money | wrong answer costs real dollars, compounding | retirement, tax, immigration fees |
| Official records | wrong answer becomes *your permanent paperwork* | immigration forms, licenses, benefits |
| Someone else's decision | a real person will act on your relayed AI word | advice to a friend with stakes |

Each of these shares a triplet:

1. **Personal specificity** — the true answer depends on your exact facts,
   which the model doesn't have.
2. **High cost of wrongness** — not recoverable by editing the document
   later.
3. **A real human whose job is to be right here** — who carries both the
   license (and its liability) and the *duty* to check the current,
   specific version of the answer. That last one matters twice, because
   it's the whole point of the license system: someone is *paid to be
   responsible*, which is exactly the thing a chatbot can never be.

## How to use AI inside the blacklist (the copilot version)

There's a real, warm way AI helps after the line — and it looks like
*preparation*:

- **Vocabulary decode.** "What does 'non-revocable trust' mean in
  everyday terms?" — reading aid, not verdict.
- **Question preparation.** "Here's my situation. What should I ask my
  doctor / the attorney about?" Ten better questions in.
- **Paperwork organization.** "Organize these visit notes into a
  timeline" — local-first (chapter 06) if anything is private.
- **Plain-English transcription.** Reading a form, a policy, a letter —
  and *flagging the sentences to ask a human about*, which is what it's
  best at.

The output of every one of these is a *better human conversation* — not
a conclusion. If your note to the professional is better prepared because
of the model, the copilot earned its seat. If your note replaced the
professionals, the tool got into the cockpit.

## The one question that draws the line in any gray moment

> **"Am I about to let this decide, or help me prepare?"**

If "help me prepare" — write on, healthy. If "decide" — stop, and route
to a person licensed to carry the outcome. That's the whole habit.
Pulling it off requires nothing more than noticing you leaned on the chat
for a *decision* — in which case, calm restart: open a new tab to the
professional, and reuse everything you learned here on the road there.

Between chapters: the model stays great for everything you happily leave
in its hands. Keep it — the blacklist is small, and the checked parts of
the map are yours to travel freely.
