# Glossary

Plain-English definitions, alphabetical. Where a term is covered in
depth, the chapter number follows.

**Correlated error**
The trickiest blind spot of the second-opinion method (chapter 06): two
AI models can share training lineage and the same mistakes, so their
agreement is weaker evidence than it feels. A difference between them is
the useful signal; sameness still needs a real source.

**Cutoff (date)**
The day a model's training data ends. Everything after — new prices, new
rules, this week's menu — is invisible to it, and it feels no different
from everything before. Where staleness checks come from (chapters
01, 02, 05).

**Exact-quote search**
The fifteen-second check: copy a claimed quote, paper title, or
source name, wrap it in quotation marks, and search. Real things surface
in seconds; fabricated ones surface as thin air. The single highest-value
move in chapter 05.

**Fabrication (invented detail)**
Taxonomy mistake #1 (chapter 02): a specific claim that simply is not
true, produced by a model filling knowledge gaps with fitting words.
Sound exactly like true claims; only checking separates them.

**Grounding**
Answering from an actual source the AI can read (a document you attached,
a live search) rather than from memory alone. Grounded answers still
need spot checks, but they give the check somewhere solid to land.

**Hallucination**
The widely used name for AI confidently generating false or invented
content. Chapter 01 argues the more honest mental model: a
pattern-completion engine filling empty shelves — not a broken person.
Useful word, slightly misleading picture.

**Independence (of sources)**
Two sources count separately only if neither is just repeating the other.
Three genuinely independent sources earns "safe to repeat in public"
(chapter 05); two articles citing the same press release is one source in
two outfits.

**Licensing (professional)**
The system by which a human — nurse, doctor, attorney, CPA — carries
legal responsibility for being right, current, and specific. AI can
never hold that responsibility, which is the whole reason for chapter
07's blacklist.

**Load-bearing detail**
The one-to-five items inside an answer that you'll actually act on or
repeat: names, numbers, dates, citations, instructions. The only things
the verification loop (chapter 04) needs to check.

**Local AI / local model**
An AI model that runs entirely on your own computer — offline, private,
no per-use cost (chapter 06). Same species as cloud chatbots; different
home.

**Ollama / LM Studio / Jan**
Free, beginner-friendly apps for running local models (chapter 06). Pick
one, download a small model, turn Wi-Fi off, ask, compare.

**Open book / closed book**
Shorthand for grounded vs. memory-only answering (see *Grounding*).
Closed-book answers on rare, recent, or numeric topics are where the
taxonomy's mistakes live most densely.

**Primary source**
The actual document: the government form page, the manufacturer's
manual, the journal paper itself. Ranks above every paraphrase of it
(chapter 05), including the best-written AI answer.

**Procedural drift**
Taxonomy mistake #10 (chapter 02): step instructions that describe a
screen that no longer (or never) exists, because software outpaces
training data. The catch: trust the screen in your hand.

**Row 1 / Row 2 / Row 3 / Row 4**
The stakes-test shorthand from chapter 03: skim freely / skim with a
numbers pass / verify before trusting / never delegate the decision.
The who-pays question assigns your ask to a row in seconds.

**Second opinion (AI)**
Asking a *different* model the same neutral question and comparing
load-bearing details (chapter 06). Disagreement is a detector that says
where to check; agreement is weak evidence and never a final word.

**Sycophancy**
The model's trained-in pull toward agreeing with your framing (chapters
01, 02; taxonomy #8), visible in drill 5. Counter: neutral questions
and "I don't have an opinion yet."

**Taxonomy (the mistake taxonomy)**
Chapter 02's field guide: twelve recurring mistake types of AI answers,
each with its look, its cause, and its cheap catch.

**Triage (answer triage)**
The verify-before-you-trust habit operating in miniature: matching
checking effort to stakes so 90% of answers flow through unchecked and
the risky 10% get real scrutiny (chapters 03, 04).

**Unresolved**
A legitimate verdict. When sources contradict or evidence runs thin,
saying "I don't know yet" beats picking a winner for comfort — in this
guide, in its two companions, and everywhere they meet.
