# The Verify-Before-You-Trust Habit

This chapter is the engine of the whole manual. Everything before it
explained the problem; everything after it adds tools and caveats. This
one builds the core habit — and it's built to take **minutes**, because a
verification habit that costs an hour a day gets abandoned by Thursday.

## The habit in one line

> Find the load-bearing details. Check those. Let the rest be pleasant.

## What a "load-bearing detail" is

Most AI answers are 90% supporting material — warm-up, framing,
qualifiers, summary. Deep inside, two to five items actually carry the
weight: the specific facts that, if wrong, change what you'll do or say.
You only ever need to check *those*.

| Load-bearing detail | Example inside an answer |
| --- | --- |
| Names | "the surgeon general," "your state's form 440," a product model |
| Numbers | a dose, a fee, a deadline, "three days," a temperature |
| Dates | a cutoff, an expiration, "since 2019," an appointment window |
| Citations | the study, the case, the quote, the manual |
| Instructions | "delete this folder," "stop taking it," "call this number" |

Finding them is fast because they're exactly the bits you'd naturally
highlight if you were making a cheat sheet. Ask yourself: **"what part of
this answer will I act on or repeat?"** — every one of those is
load-bearing.

## The four-step verification loop

This is the whole habit. It runs in one to five minutes, once you've read
the answer once for usefulness (that read is free — you were going to do
it anyway).

### Step 1: Assign the row (five seconds)

Chapter 03's question — *if this is wrong, who pays?* — runs instantly
now. Row 1–2 answers never enter this loop at all. You're here for row 3s.

### Step 2: Isolate the load-bearing details (thirty seconds)

Underline, mentally or actually: every name, number, date, citation, or
instruction you'll act on. If the list is longer than five items, that's a
sign the answer is actually row 4 or you're near a decision that deserves
a human — no AI answer was ever that packed with independently important
facts and *also* trustworthy.

### Step 3: Check by type (one to four minutes)

Match each load-bearing item to its cheap, targeted check — the taxonomy's
"catch" column, made concrete in chapter 05:

- **Fabricated fact** → exact-quote search, one real source
- **Stale fact** → ask the *source*, not the model, for the current value
- **Numeric slip** → recompute; count the list yourself
- **Invented citation** → search the title in quotation marks
- **Instruction** → confirm against the actual screen/manual

For the commonest case, this is one new browser tab and one search.

### Step 4: Trust forward (ten seconds)

If the load-bearing details check out, **use the answer with a clear
conscience** — including the parts you didn't check, which are now
"supporting color" rather than claims. If one detail failed, you don't
need to distrust everything: tell the model what failed, ask it to redo
that piece, and re-check just the redo. Small repair beats full re-build,
and the repair loop is where AI actually gets useful, honestly — including
when it's wrong.

:::tip What you get back for the minutes
Every verified answer pays you twice: once now, once later when it comes
up for "wait, I know this one" — because you checked, the answer in your
head is the one that's actually true. The more you verify before
repeating, the more your *own* word gets to be trusted by everyone around
you, including you.
:::

## The two fastest checks, for the impatient

If you're in a hurry, two checks cover a shocking amount:

**The exact-quote search.** Any citation, quote, or specific factual
sentence gets copied out, wrapped in quotation marks, and searched. This
takes fifteen seconds and catches mistakes #4, #5 (chapter 02), and most
fabricated facts that carry a specific string of words.

**The second model.** Paste the same neutral question into a second AI —
an assistant on another service, or a local model on your own machine
(chapter 06) — and compare the *load-bearing details* only. Agreement is
weak evidence (they may share training data), but disagreement is
excellent information: it tells you *where* the checking must go and
often that the answer was never solid enough to repeat.

## What the habit is not

**Not** checking everything. The entire design is to let 90% of answers
through unchecked, so the 10% that matter get real scrutiny. A habit with
no triage is a habit that fails within a week.

**Not** a ritual for its own sake. If the search changes nothing — same
answer, same confidence — the loop made your trust *earned* rather than
given. That's the win, even when the answer was already right.

**Not** a test of the model's character. The model will keep answering
perfectly, confidently, helpfully — including when wrong. That's not an
insult to you or a flaw you need to fix. It's a fact about the tool
(chapter 01), and this habit is how you use a fluent tool without getting
worn down by it.

## Training drills (small, deliberate, weekly)

Chapter 09 has a full drill set, but two minutes here establishes why
practice deserves presence of mind:

- The habit only works if it fires when *it matters* — that means firing
  on high-stakes asks alone is not enough: rehearse with low stakes until
  the tempo of "skim vs. check" feels like one motion, not two
  chores.
- Resistance wears in once, then keeps: after a week of drills, you'll
  find yourself wondering "which load-bearing detail here?" without
  deciding to.
